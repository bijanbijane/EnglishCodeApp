# Sample Lesson — A2: Talk About a Personal Goal

**Status:** Original EnglishCode sample; ready for editorial review, not yet a validated placement or certification instrument.

**Communicative outcome:** Describe one familiar personal goal, give a simple reason, and explain two next steps in 5–7 connected sentences.

**Real-world task:** Send a 45–60 second voice message to a study partner about one goal for the next month.

## 1. Context and purpose

Choose one goal that matters in everyday life. The listener should understand what the goal is, why it matters, and what the speaker will do next.

Possible topics:
- Improve English speaking.
- Walk more regularly.
- Learn to cook two new meals.
- Organize a study routine.

## 2. Comprehensible input

**Model exchange**

**Maya:** What do you want to do next month?

**Sam:** I want to improve my English speaking.

**Maya:** Why is that important to you?

**Sam:** Because I need English for work, and I want to feel more confident.

**Maya:** What is your plan?

**Sam:** First, I am planning to speak for ten minutes every day. Then I want to record one short voice message each evening.

**Maya:** That sounds realistic. When will you start?

**Sam:** I will start on Monday.

**Input tasks**
1. Identify Sam's goal.
2. Find the reason.
3. List the two actions in the plan.
4. Listen again and notice how the speaker connects the ideas.

## 3. Noticing the language

| Function | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| State a goal | want to + base verb | I want to improve my speaking. |
| State an intention | am planning to + base verb | I am planning to practise daily. |
| Give a reason | because + clause | Because I need English at work. |
| Sequence actions | first / then | First, I will listen. Then, I will speak. |

**Common form issue:** Use the base form after `want to` and `planning to`: “I want to practise”, not “I want to practising”.

## 4. Guided practice

Complete the sentences with your own information.

1. I want to ______ because ______.
2. I am planning to ______ this week.
3. First, I will ______. Then, I will ______.

Choose the correct form:
- I want to **speak / speaking** more clearly.
- I am planning to **study / studying** after work.
- I want to **learn / learning** five useful phrases.

Put the plan in order:
- Then, I will record a short message.
- First, I will choose a topic.

**Answer key:** speak; study; learn. First choose a topic; then record a message.

## 5. Independent production

Record a 45–60 second voice message. Include:
- One clear personal goal.
- One reason.
- Two realistic next steps in a logical order.

Use keywords only. Do not read a fully written script.

## 6. Feedback and retry

Use a short evidence-based feedback cycle:
1. Name one specific success from the message.
2. Select one high-value issue that affects clarity or target-language accuracy.
3. Give one actionable instruction.
4. Ask the learner to record a second version.

Do not correct every minor error at once. Prioritize the lesson objective and intelligibility.

## 7. Delayed retrieval

- **After about 24 hours:** Describe the same goal without looking at notes.
- **After 3 days:** Rebuild the message from three keywords.
- **After 7 days:** Describe a different goal using the same language functions.

These intervals are a practical starting schedule, not a claim that one fixed schedule is optimal for every learner. Adjust based on retrieval success and learner workload.

## 8. Assessment and mastery gate

Score each dimension from 0 to 2.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task completion | Missing key ideas | Some ideas included | Goal, reason, and two steps are clear |
| Connected speech | Isolated or unclear phrases | Some connection | Ideas are linked understandably |
| Target forms | Frequent blocking errors | Partly controlled | Target forms are used appropriately |
| Independent transfer | Needs full prompts | Needs keywords | Completes a new-goal task independently |

**Mastery rule:** At least 6/8 (75%) is not enough on its own. The learner must also complete a second attempt and demonstrate delayed recall with a different goal. The blueprint's default 80% gate applies to the lesson's scored assessment; teachers should review rubric calibration before using it for consequential decisions.

## Design rationale

This lesson combines communicative purpose, comprehensible model input, focused noticing, controlled practice, meaningful production, selective feedback, retrieval, and delayed transfer. It is designed as an original CEFR-informed A2 practice sample. It does not reproduce Council of Europe descriptor wording or third-party textbook material.

The lesson blueprint and its eight required stages are validated by `validateLessonBlueprint` in the dependency-free learning-core package.
