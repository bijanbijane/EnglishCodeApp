# A1 Foundations — Unit 2: Everyday Objects and Classroom Things

**Status:** Authored curriculum increment  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Prerequisite:** Unit 1 — First Conversations  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only; no publisher lesson text, dictionary definitions or proprietary exercises are reproduced.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR alignment:** identify familiar everyday objects, ask and answer simple questions, describe basic features and locations, and make a simple polite request.
- **Vocabulary selection:** high-frequency concrete nouns, colours and spatial words introduced in short, reusable phrases.
- **Grammar sequence:** demonstratives and articles → regular plurals → adjective position and be → basic prepositions → can I…? requests and possessive adjectives.
- **Method:** concrete objects or picture prompts → model phrase → noticing → controlled retrieval → paired classroom task.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic recognition questions per lesson. These do not constitute complete speaking, listening, reading or writing assessment.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. name familiar classroom and personal objects;
2. ask what one or more objects are;
3. describe an object with a basic colour or size adjective;
4. say where an object is using common prepositions;
5. ask politely to borrow an object and respond to thanks.

## Lesson 1 — What's This?

**Can-do:** I can identify a familiar object near me or farther away.

**Core vocabulary:** pen, pencil, notebook, book, bag, ruler, eraser, desk.

**Language focus:**
- What's this? — It's a pen.
- What's that? — It's a notebook.
- a book / an eraser

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What's this?  
> B: It's a notebook.  
> A: And what's that?  
> B: It's a ruler.

**Teaching notes:** Demonstrate distance physically. Use *this* for one nearby object and *that* for one farther away. Teach *a/an* by initial sound, not initial letter.

**Communicative task:** Point to six classroom objects. Ask and answer “What's this?” or “What's that?”

## Lesson 2 — Things Around You

**Can-do:** I can name more than one familiar object.

**Core vocabulary:** books, bags, pencils, boxes, watches, keys, phones, chairs.

**Language focus:**
- one book → two books
- one box → two boxes
- What are these? — They're keys.
- What are those? — They're boxes.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What are these?  
> B: They're keys.  
> A: And what are those?  
> B: They're boxes.

**Teaching notes:** Most regular nouns add *-s*. Many nouns ending in *-x* or *-ch* add *-es*. Contrast *this/that* (singular) with *these/those* (plural).

**Communicative task:** Sort picture cards into one object and more than one. Ask and answer with demonstratives.

## Lesson 3 — Colours and Descriptions

**Can-do:** I can describe a familiar object with a colour or size adjective.

**Core vocabulary:** red, blue, green, black, white, yellow, big, small, new, old.

**Language focus:**
- a blue bag
- The bag is blue.
- The pencils are green.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Is your bag new?  
> B: No, it's old. It's black.  
> A: My bag is small and blue.

**Teaching notes:** An adjective usually comes before a noun (*a blue bag*) or after *be* (*The bag is blue*). Adjectives do not take plural *-s*.

**Communicative task:** Describe a picture card using a colour and a size adjective. A partner identifies it.

## Lesson 4 — Where Is It?

**Can-do:** I can ask where an object is and say where it is.

**Core vocabulary:** in, on, under, next to, desk, chair, table, bag, book, pencil case.

**Language focus:**
- Where is my notebook? — It's on the desk.
- Where's my pencil case? — It's in my bag.
- The bag is under the chair.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Where's my pencil case?  
> B: It's in your bag.  
> A: And my notebook?  
> B: It's on the desk.

**Teaching notes:** Teach prepositions through physical demonstration. Contrast *in*, *on*, *under* and *next to* with real objects or pictures.

**Communicative task:** Place three objects in, on, under or next to classroom furniture. A partner asks where each object is.

## Lesson 5 — Can I Borrow That?

**Can-do:** I can ask politely to borrow an everyday object and respond to a simple request.

**Core vocabulary:** borrow, please, here you are, thank you, you're welcome, my, your, his, her.

**Language focus:**
- Can I borrow your pen, please?
- Of course. Here you are.
- Thank you. — You're welcome.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Can I borrow your pen, please?  
> B: Of course. Here you are.  
> A: Thank you.  
> B: You're welcome.

**Teaching notes:** Practise *Can I…?* as a request, not a statement of ability. Use *your* for something belonging to the person being addressed.

**Communicative task:** Role-play a classroom situation. Ask to borrow an object, hand it over, and thank the other person.
