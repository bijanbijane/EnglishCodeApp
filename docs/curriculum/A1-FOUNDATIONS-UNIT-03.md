# A1 Foundations — Unit 3: Family and Friends

**Status:** Authored curriculum increment  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Prerequisite:** Units 1–2 — First Conversations; Everyday Objects and Classroom Things  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only; no publisher lesson text, dictionary definitions or proprietary exercises are reproduced.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR alignment:** give simple information about close family and familiar people, describe a person in basic terms, and exchange simple information about likes.
- **Vocabulary selection:** high-utility family terms, age expressions, basic appearance adjectives and common preference vocabulary.
- **Grammar sequence:** possessive adjectives → age questions and be → have got → like/likes and Do-questions → possessive 's and whose.
- **Method:** familiar-person context → modelled language → guided noticing → controlled retrieval → short pair exchange.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic recognition questions per lesson. This is not a complete speaking or listening assessment.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. name close family members and state simple relationships;
2. ask and answer how old familiar people are;
3. describe a person's basic appearance;
4. say what they and another person like;
5. ask who owns a familiar object.

## Lesson 1 — My Family

**Can-do:** I can name close family members and say who someone is.

**Core vocabulary:** mother, father, parents, sister, brother, son, daughter, grandmother, grandfather, aunt, uncle, cousin.

**Language focus:**
- This is my sister.
- He is my brother.
- They are my parents.
- my / your / his / her

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Who is she?  
> B: She's my sister, Leyla.  
> A: And who are they?  
> B: They're my parents.

**Teaching notes:** Teach family words through a fictional family tree. Use possessive adjectives before nouns (*my sister*, *his father*). Do not ask learners to disclose private family details; fictional profiles are sufficient.

**Communicative task:** Use a fictional family tree to introduce three people.

## Lesson 2 — How Old Are They?

**Can-do:** I can ask and answer about a familiar person's age.

**Core vocabulary:** age, how old, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, one hundred.

**Language focus:**
- How old is your brother? — He's twenty.
- How old are your parents? — They're fifty.
- She is thirty-two.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: How old is your grandmother?  
> B: She's seventy.  
> A: And your grandfather?  
> B: He's seventy-two.

**Teaching notes:** Use *is* with one person and *are* with plural subjects. Practise hearing and distinguishing numbers in the 20–100 range; later listening tasks should test these contrasts in speech.

**Communicative task:** Ask and answer age questions about a set of fictional profile cards.

## Lesson 3 — Describing People

**Can-do:** I can give a simple description of someone's appearance.

**Core vocabulary:** tall, short, young, old, friendly, kind, hair, eyes, long, short, brown, black.

**Language focus:**
- He's tall. / She's friendly.
- He has got short hair.
- She has got brown eyes.
- They have got …

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What does your friend look like?  
> B: She's tall. She's got long, dark hair.  
> A: Is she friendly?  
> B: Yes, she is.

**Teaching notes:** Teach *be + adjective* for general descriptions and *have/has got + noun phrase* for features. Keep descriptions neutral and respectful; do not use appearance as a basis for negative judgement.

**Communicative task:** Describe a fictional character card; a partner selects the matching card.

## Lesson 4 — Friends and Likes

**Can-do:** I can say what I like and ask another person about preferences.

**Core vocabulary:** music, football, books, films, tea, coffee, food, games, like, don't like.

**Language focus:**
- I like music. / I don't like coffee.
- She likes books.
- Do you like football? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Do you like music?  
> B: Yes, I do. I like pop music.  
> A: Does your friend like music?  
> B: Yes, she does.

**Teaching notes:** In the present simple, use *like* with I/you/we/they and *likes* with he/she/it. Questions with *do* use the base verb: *Do you like …?* Later lessons can extend to *does* questions.

**Communicative task:** Complete a fictional preference survey and report one result about a partner.

## Lesson 5 — Whose Is It?

**Can-do:** I can ask who owns an object and identify its owner.

**Core vocabulary:** whose, belong, phone, bag, book, key, owner, parents.

**Language focus:**
- Whose phone is this?
- It's Sara's phone.
- These are my parents' keys.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Whose book is this?  
> B: It's Amir's book.  
> A: And whose keys are these?  
> B: They're my parents' keys.

**Teaching notes:** Use apostrophe + s for one owner (*Sara's phone*). For a regular plural ending in -s, place the apostrophe after the s (*my parents' keys*). Contrast *whose* (ownership) with *who's* (who is).

**Communicative task:** Match fictional name cards to object cards and ask “Whose … is this?”
