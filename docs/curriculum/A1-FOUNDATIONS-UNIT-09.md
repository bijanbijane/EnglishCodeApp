# A1 Foundations — Unit 9: Practical Places, Travel and Services

**Status:** Authored curriculum increment  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Prerequisite:** Units 1–8  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR alignment:** handle simple, predictable exchanges when travelling; ask for and understand basic information about transport, tickets and accommodation.
- **Vocabulary:** public transport, station facilities, tickets, departure/arrival, hotel facilities and basic travel information.
- **Grammar and functions:** by + transport, at + clock time, how much/how long/where, polite requests with can/could, next to, present continuous for arrangements and simple going-to plans.
- **Method:** travel-sign visuals → phrase rehearsal → modelled service encounters → controlled question practice → short role-play with information cards.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic recognition questions per lesson. Real-time listening, role-play and reading timetables require dedicated task formats beyond these MCQs.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. name common forms of transport and say how they travel;
2. recognise basic station vocabulary and ask about times;
3. request a simple ticket and understand single/return distinctions;
4. use basic phrases when checking into a hotel;
5. ask for simple travel information and describe a basic journey.

## Lesson 1 — Getting Around Town

**Can-do:** I can name common transport and say how I travel.

**Core vocabulary:** bus, train, tram, underground, taxi, car, bicycle, boat, station, stop, airport.

**Language focus:** by bus/train/car; on foot; I go to work by bus; How do you get to school?

**Model dialogue:**
> A: How do you get to school?  
> B: I go by bus.  
> A: Is the bus stop near your home?  
> B: Yes, it's next to the shop.

**Teaching notes:** Distinguish by bus/train from on foot. Transport systems and labels differ by city; include locally relevant examples.

**Communicative task:** Match transport pictures to short journey descriptions and tell a partner how you travel.

## Lesson 2 — At the Station

**Can-do:** I can understand simple station signs and ask about departure times.

**Core vocabulary:** station, ticket office, platform, timetable, departure, arrival, train, bus, late, early.

**Language focus:** What time does it leave?; It leaves at 10:30; Which platform?; The train arrives at noon.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Excuse me, what time does the train leave?  
> B: At 10:30.  
> A: Which platform?  
> B: Platform three.

**Teaching notes:** Use a simplified timetable with a small number of services. Teach at with exact clock times and keep leave/arrive meanings distinct.

**Communicative task:** Read a simple timetable and answer when and where one service departs.

## Lesson 3 — Buying Tickets

**Can-do:** I can ask for a simple ticket and understand basic ticket information.

**Core vocabulary:** ticket, single, return, adult, child, price, one-way, journey, please.

**Language focus:** A single ticket to ... , please; How much is it?; Can I pay by card?; I'd like a return ticket.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Hello. A return ticket to the city centre, please.  
> B: Certainly.  
> A: How much is it?  
> B: It's twelve euros.

**Teaching notes:** Use fictional sample prices and currencies in teaching materials. Explain that ticket types and payment methods vary by transport operator.

**Communicative task:** Role-play a ticket purchase using destination and ticket-type cards.

## Lesson 4 — At a Hotel

**Can-do:** I can use simple phrases when checking in and asking for something I need.

**Core vocabulary:** hotel, reservation, room, key card, reception, breakfast, towel, lift/elevator, floor.

**Language focus:** I have a reservation; Could I have ... , please?; Is breakfast included?; Where is the lift?

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Hello. I have a reservation.  
> B: What's your name, please?  
> A: It's ...  
> B: Here is your key card.

**Teaching notes:** Teach lift/elevator as regional alternatives. Practise polite requests as chunks; do not require learners to master conditional grammar to use them.

**Communicative task:** Use a simple hotel information card to ask three practical questions.

## Lesson 5 — Travel Information and Simple Plans

**Can-do:** I can ask where a service goes and describe a simple travel plan.

**Core vocabulary:** destination, bus stop, airport, city centre, next to, near, far, journey, take, minutes.

**Language focus:** Where does this bus go?; How long does it take?; The stop is next to ...; We are going to the airport tomorrow.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Excuse me, where does this bus go?  
> B: To the airport.  
> A: How long does it take?  
> B: About twenty minutes.

**Teaching notes:** Use simple route maps and clear landmarks. Distinguish journey duration from departure time. Keep going-to as a useful travel-plan chunk.

**Communicative task:** Read a simple route card, ask for the destination and estimate the journey duration.
