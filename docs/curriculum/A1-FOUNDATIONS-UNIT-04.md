# A1 Foundations — Unit 4: Daily Routines and Time

**Status:** Authored curriculum increment  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Prerequisite:** Units 1–3 — First Conversations; Everyday Objects; Family and Friends  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only; no publisher lesson text, dictionary definitions or proprietary exercises are reproduced.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR alignment:** describe familiar daily activities, understand and give simple times, and ask basic questions about routines.
- **Vocabulary selection:** common routine verbs, clock-time expressions and days of the week.
- **Grammar sequence:** present simple with I/you → clock times and at/on → third-person singular forms → Do/Does questions and short answers.
- **Method:** daily timeline → modelled exchange → noticing of verb forms → controlled practice → pair interview.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic recognition questions per lesson. Listening to spoken time expressions and spontaneous speaking require additional tasks.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. describe a simple daily routine;
2. understand and say common clock times;
3. name days of the week and say when an activity happens;
4. describe another person's routine using he/she forms;
5. ask and answer simple questions about a daily schedule.

## Lesson 1 — My Day

**Can-do:** I can talk about a few things I do every day.

**Core vocabulary:** wake up, get up, wash, have breakfast, go to school, go to work, come home, have dinner, go to bed.

**Language focus:**
- I wake up at seven.
- I have breakfast at seven thirty.
- I go to work in the morning.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What do you do in the morning?  
> B: I get up at seven. I have breakfast and go to work.  
> A: What time do you come home?  
> B: I come home at six.

**Teaching notes:** Present activities in a simple visual timeline. Contrast wake up (stop sleeping) with get up (leave the bed). Use the base verb with I.

**Communicative task:** Put five routine picture cards in order and describe the sequence using I.

## Lesson 2 — What Time Is It?

**Can-do:** I can understand and say common clock times.

**Core vocabulary:** o'clock, half past, quarter past, quarter to, minute, hour.

**Language focus:**
- It's seven o'clock.
- It's half past seven.
- It's quarter past eight.
- It's quarter to ten.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What time is it?  
> B: It's half past seven.  
> A: And what time is the lesson?  
> B: It's at quarter past eight.

**Teaching notes:** Use analogue clock faces and digital time together. Teach quarter past as 15 minutes after the hour, half past as 30 minutes after, and quarter to as 15 minutes before the next hour.

**Communicative task:** Match six clock faces to written time expressions, then ask and answer “What time is it?”

## Lesson 3 — A Busy Week

**Can-do:** I can say what day an activity happens.

**Core vocabulary:** Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, weekday, weekend.

**Language focus:**
- on Monday
- I study English on Tuesday.
- I visit my friend at the weekend.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: When do you study English?  
> B: I study English on Monday and Wednesday.  
> A: What do you do at the weekend?  
> B: I visit my family.

**Teaching notes:** Capitalize day names. Use on with days. Regional varieties differ in weekend phrasing; teach one consistent model first and note alternatives later.

**Communicative task:** Complete a fictional weekly timetable and tell a partner about two activities.

## Lesson 4 — He Gets Up Early

**Can-do:** I can describe another person's daily routine.

**Core vocabulary:** early, late, starts, finishes, gets up, goes, has, works, studies.

**Language focus:**
- I get up at seven.
- She gets up at six.
- He goes to work at eight.
- They have breakfast at home.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What time does Sara get up?  
> B: She gets up at six thirty.  
> A: Does she walk to work?  
> B: Yes, she does.

**Teaching notes:** Add -s or -es to most he/she/it present-simple affirmative verbs. Some forms change spelling (*have → has*, *go → goes*). In Does-questions, use the base verb: “Does she walk?”, not “Does she walks?”

**Communicative task:** Compare two fictional daily schedules and report one difference.

## Lesson 5 — Asking About Routines

**Can-do:** I can ask someone about their schedule and understand a short answer.

**Core vocabulary:** what time, when, start, finish, walk, bus, usually, every day.

**Language focus:**
- What time do you get up? — At seven.
- Do you walk to school? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
- What time does she start? — At nine.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What time do you start work?  
> B: I start at nine.  
> A: Do you go by bus?  
> B: No, I walk.

**Teaching notes:** Use do with I/you/we/they and does with he/she/it. After do/does, use the base verb. Keep short answers consistent with the question auxiliary.

**Communicative task:** Interview a partner using three routine questions, then report one answer.
