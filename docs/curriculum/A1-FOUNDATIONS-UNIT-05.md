# A1 Foundations — Unit 5: Food, Drinks and Preferences

**Status:** Authored curriculum increment  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Prerequisite:** Units 1–4  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR alignment:** understand and use familiar food and drink words; express likes and dislikes; make simple requests and ask about prices.
- **Vocabulary:** common fruit, vegetables, meals, drinks, café words, containers and quantities.
- **Grammar and functions:** a/an, plural nouns, like/don't like, Do-questions, I'd like, How much.
- **Method:** visual menu and food groups → modelled exchange → noticing → controlled practice → role-play.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic recognition questions per lesson. Real listening and open-ended speaking/writing need separate assessment activities.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. name common fruit, vegetables, meals and drinks;
2. talk simply about food preferences;
3. order a snack or drink politely;
4. understand basic menu and price questions;
5. ask for common quantities in a shop.

## Lesson 1 — Fruit and Vegetables

**Can-do:** I can name some common fruit and vegetables.

**Core vocabulary:** apple, banana, orange, pear, grape, carrot, potato, tomato, onion, cucumber.

**Language focus:** a banana; an apple; two bananas; I eat an apple.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What fruit do you like?  
> B: I like apples and bananas.  
> A: Do you like carrots?  
> B: No, I don't.

**Teaching notes:** Teach fruit and vegetables with clear picture sorting. Contrast a/an by initial sound, not spelling alone. Introduce plural -s and common spelling changes such as tomato → tomatoes.

**Communicative task:** Sort a small illustrated shopping list into fruit and vegetables, then name three items.

## Lesson 2 — Meals and Drinks

**Can-do:** I can talk about simple meals and drinks.

**Core vocabulary:** breakfast, lunch, dinner, bread, rice, soup, salad, water, tea, coffee, milk, juice.

**Language focus:** I have breakfast; I drink water; for breakfast; for lunch.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What do you have for breakfast?  
> B: I have bread and cheese. I drink tea.  
> A: What about lunch?  
> B: I have rice and salad.

**Teaching notes:** Avoid implying that one meal pattern is universal. Use neutral examples and allow learners to substitute familiar foods.

**Communicative task:** Choose food and a drink for a simple breakfast, then describe the choice.

## Lesson 3 — At the Café

**Can-do:** I can order a simple drink or snack politely.

**Core vocabulary:** menu, table, sandwich, tea, coffee, water, please, thank you.

**Language focus:** I'd like a coffee, please; Can I have a sandwich, please?; Here you are.

**Model dialogue:**
> Server: Hello. What would you like?  
> Customer: I'd like a tea and a sandwich, please.  
> Server: Here you are.  
> Customer: Thank you.

**Teaching notes:** Model intonation and polite turn-taking. Teach I'd like as a useful fixed request frame before explaining its grammar in detail.

**Communicative task:** Role-play customer and server using a short fictional menu.

## Lesson 4 — Likes and Dislikes

**Can-do:** I can say what food and drink I like or dislike.

**Core vocabulary:** like, love, don't like, favourite, food, drink.

**Language focus:** I like rice; I don't like coffee; Do you like tea? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Do you like fish?  
> B: Yes, I do.  
> A: Do you like coffee?  
> B: No, I don't. I like tea.

**Teaching notes:** Use do/don't with I and you. Keep preferences personal and non-judgemental; do not frame one cuisine or diet as the norm.

**Communicative task:** Complete a preference chart and report two choices to a partner.

## Lesson 5 — Shopping for Food

**Can-do:** I can ask for simple quantities and prices.

**Core vocabulary:** shop, market, price, kilo, bottle, packet, loaf, how much, two, some.

**Language focus:** How much is it?; How much are the apples?; a bottle of water; two bananas; I'd like a kilo of oranges.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Can I help you?  
> B: Yes, I'd like a kilo of apples, please.  
> A: Anything else?  
> B: A bottle of water, please. How much is it?

**Teaching notes:** Teach common measure phrases as chunks. Distinguish How much is it? for price from How many apples? for countable items; extend this contrast in later A1 review.

**Communicative task:** Use a fictional price list to buy three items and ask for the total.
