# A1 Foundations — Unit 6: Home, Places and Directions

**Status:** Authored curriculum increment  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Prerequisite:** Units 1–5  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR alignment:** identify familiar places, describe a simple home, locate objects and places, and understand short directions.
- **Vocabulary:** rooms, furniture, public places, location words, transport and direction verbs.
- **Grammar and functions:** there is / there are, singular/plural nouns, in/on/under/next to/between/behind, where-questions and imperative directions, by + transport.
- **Method:** labelled home/town maps → modelled language → guided noticing → controlled practice → information-gap route task.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic recognition questions per lesson. Map-reading, listening and spoken route-giving require separate skill activities.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. name common rooms and furniture;
2. identify familiar places in a town;
3. say where an object or place is using basic prepositions;
4. ask for and follow very simple directions;
5. name common transport and describe a short journey.

## Lesson 1 — Rooms and Furniture

**Can-do:** I can name rooms and common things in a home.

**Core vocabulary:** house, flat, room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, living room, table, chair, bed, sofa, wardrobe.

**Language focus:** There is a kitchen; There are two bedrooms; a table; an armchair.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Is there a table in the kitchen?  
> B: Yes, there is.  
> A: Are there chairs, too?  
> B: Yes, there are four chairs.

**Teaching notes:** Use a simple floor plan. Teach room names before furniture and distinguish singular there is from plural there are.

**Communicative task:** Label a home plan and describe three rooms.

## Lesson 2 — Places in Town

**Can-do:** I can recognise common places and say what people do there.

**Core vocabulary:** bank, bus stop, café, hospital, library, market, museum, park, pharmacy, post office, school, station, supermarket.

**Language focus:** Where is the library?; It is near the park; You can buy bread at a bakery.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Where is the pharmacy?  
> B: It is next to the bank.  
> A: Thank you.  
> B: You're welcome.

**Teaching notes:** Use a consistent town map. Avoid assuming every learner has the same local services or transport system.

**Communicative task:** Match six places to simple purposes, then locate two on a map.

## Lesson 3 — Where Is It?

**Can-do:** I can describe where familiar objects and places are.

**Core vocabulary:** in, on, under, next to, behind, in front of, between, near.

**Language focus:** There is a shop; There are two parks; The bag is under the chair; The bank is between the café and the library.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Where is my phone?  
> B: It is on the table.  
> A: And my keys?  
> B: They are next to the bag.

**Teaching notes:** Demonstrate prepositions with classroom objects. Contrast in front of and behind with clear spatial visuals.

**Communicative task:** One learner places objects on a picture; another describes their positions.

## Lesson 4 — Asking for and Giving Directions

**Can-do:** I can ask where a place is and understand a few simple directions.

**Core vocabulary:** go straight, turn left, turn right, stop, cross, street, corner, traffic lights, crossing.

**Language focus:** Excuse me, where is the station?; Go straight; Turn left at the corner; It is on your right.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Excuse me, how can I get to the museum?  
> B: Go straight and turn right at the traffic lights.  
> A: Is it far?  
> B: No, it is near the park.

**Teaching notes:** Keep routes short and use a high-contrast map. Teach direction phrases as chunks before extending to longer instructions. Include pedestrian safety in route tasks.

**Communicative task:** Follow a two-step route on a town map and tell a partner where it ends.

## Lesson 5 — Getting Around Town

**Can-do:** I can name common transport and describe a simple journey.

**Core vocabulary:** bus, train, tram, taxi, car, bicycle, underground, stop, ticket, near, far.

**Language focus:** by bus / by train; I walk to school; The station is far from here; How do you go to work?

**Model dialogue:**
> A: How do you go to the city centre?  
> B: I go by bus.  
> A: Is the bus stop near?  
> B: Yes, it is opposite the supermarket.

**Teaching notes:** Transport options vary by location. Accept locally relevant alternatives and distinguish by bus from on the bus when describing general transport versus a position.

**Communicative task:** Choose a route between two places and explain the transport in two short sentences.
