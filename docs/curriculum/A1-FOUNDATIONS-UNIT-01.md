# A1 Foundations — Unit 1: First Conversations

**Status:** Initial authored curriculum release  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only; no publisher lesson text, dictionary definitions or proprietary exercises are reproduced.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR:** Learners exchange simple personal information, understand familiar expressions and follow short, clear instructions.
- **Oxford-informed vocabulary selection:** high-utility, beginner-appropriate lexical items taught in context. This is not a reproduction of Oxford word lists.
- **Cambridge-informed assessment:** short communicative tasks, controlled practice and clear evidence of achievement.
- **Longman-informed lexical teaching:** parts of speech, common phrase patterns and distinctions between country and nationality forms.
- **Method:** presentation → guided noticing → controlled practice → short communicative use → retrieval quiz.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic multiple-choice items per lesson. Backend owns answer keys and scoring. These items test recognition; later releases must add productive tasks and listening evidence before claiming full skill coverage.

## Unit 1 outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. greet someone at different times of day and introduce themselves;
2. ask and answer basic questions about name, age, origin and place of residence;
3. identify selected countries and nationality adjectives;
4. understand and exchange numbers and simple phone details;
5. follow common classroom instructions and request repetition or clarification.

## Lesson 1 — Hello and Introductions

**Can-do:** I can greet someone, say my name and respond to an introduction.

**Core vocabulary:** hello, hi, good morning, good afternoon, good evening, goodbye, please, thank you, name, meet.

**Language focus:**
- I am + name: I'm Sara.
- What's your name?
- Nice to meet you. / Nice to meet you, too.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Hello. I'm Sara. What's your name?  
> B: Hi, I'm Daniel. Nice to meet you.  
> A: Nice to meet you, too.

**Teaching notes:** Contrast greetings with farewells. “Good night” is generally used when leaving or before sleep, not as a daytime greeting. Practise contractions in speech while keeping the full form visible initially.

**Communicative task:** Introduce yourself to a new classmate in two or three turns.

## Lesson 2 — Personal Information

**Can-do:** I can give my age, hometown and current city.

**Core vocabulary:** first name, surname, age, old, live, city, country, from, address, phone number.

**Language focus:**
- How old are you? — I'm 20.
- Where are you from? — I'm from Azerbaijan.
- Where do you live? — I live in Baku.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Where are you from, Amir?  
> B: I'm from Azerbaijan. I live in Baku.  
> A: How old are you?  
> B: I'm 22.

**Teaching notes:** Distinguish origin (“be from”) from current residence (“live in”). Avoid “How many years are you?” Use “How old are you?”

**Communicative task:** Complete a fictional profile card and exchange three personal-information questions. Do not request real addresses or phone numbers from learners.

## Lesson 3 — Countries and Nationalities

**Can-do:** I can say where someone is from and name their nationality.

**Core vocabulary:** Azerbaijan/Azerbaijani, Turkey/Turkish, Japan/Japanese, Spain/Spanish, Brazil/Brazilian.

**Language focus:**
- She is from Japan.
- She is Japanese.
- They are from Spain.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Is Ken from Japan?  
> B: Yes, he is. He's Japanese.  
> A: And Maria?  
> B: She's from Brazil. She's Brazilian.

**Teaching notes:** Country names and nationality adjectives are capitalized. Teach country and nationality as related but different word forms. Do not generalize nationality suffixes; Japanese is not created by adding -ian.

**Communicative task:** Match five fictional people to country cards and introduce each person using “from” and the nationality adjective.

## Lesson 4 — Numbers and Contact Details

**Can-do:** I can recognize numbers 0–20 and ask someone to repeat a number.

**Core vocabulary:** numbers zero to twenty, number, phone number.

**Language focus:**
- What's your phone number?
- It's …
- Can you repeat that, please?
- I'm eighteen (years old).

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What's your phone number?  
> B: It's 555-0184.  
> A: Sorry, can you repeat that?  
> B: Five-five-five, zero-one-eight-four.

**Teaching notes:** Practise number recognition in both directions. Use fictional contact details only. Teach “zero” as a digit name and accept regional variation in telephone-number grouping in later listening material.

**Communicative task:** Read fictional number strings, write the digits, then ask for repetition.

## Lesson 5 — Classroom English

**Can-do:** I can follow simple classroom instructions and ask for clarification.

**Core vocabulary:** listen, repeat, read, write, speak, open, close, look, understand, question, answer, sorry.

**Language focus:**
- Open your book. / Listen and repeat.
- I don't understand.
- Can you repeat that, please?
- I have a question.

**Model dialogue:**
> Teacher: Open your books, please. Listen and repeat.  
> Student: Sorry, can you repeat that, please?  
> Teacher: Of course. Listen: “Good morning.”  
> Student: Good morning.

**Teaching notes:** Imperatives use the base verb. After “don't”, use the base form: “I don't understand”, not “I don't understands”. Practise polite requests with please.

**Communicative task:** Follow a sequence of three instructions and ask for repetition once when needed.
