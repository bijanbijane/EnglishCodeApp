# A1 Foundations — Unit 7: Work, Study and Free-time Activities

**Status:** Authored curriculum increment  
**Target:** CEFR A1  
**Prerequisite:** Units 1–6  
**Language pair:** Persian support / English target  
**Content rights:** Original instructional text written for EnglishCodeApp. Oxford, Cambridge and Longman are methodological references only.

## Instructional design

- **CEFR alignment:** understand and use familiar words for jobs, school, hobbies and weekly routines; exchange simple information about work and study.
- **Vocabulary:** occupations/workplaces, school subjects and classroom objects, hobbies, days and weekly activities.
- **Grammar and functions:** present simple affirmative/negative/questions, third-person singular, do/does short answers, like + gerund, preposition on + day.
- **Method:** contextual picture prompts → vocabulary retrieval → modelled mini-dialogues → controlled form practice → short learner-to-learner exchange.
- **Assessment:** four deterministic recognition questions per lesson. Spoken interaction and listening remain separate skill outcomes and need dedicated tasks.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this unit, a learner can:
1. name familiar jobs and workplaces;
2. identify common school subjects and classroom objects;
3. talk about a few hobbies and free-time preferences;
4. name days and describe a simple weekly activity;
5. ask and answer basic questions about work and study.

## Lesson 1 — Jobs and Workplaces

**Can-do:** I can name familiar jobs and say where people work.

**Core vocabulary:** doctor, nurse, teacher, student, driver, cook, chef, farmer, shop assistant, office, hospital, school, restaurant, farm.

**Language focus:** He is a doctor; She works in a hospital; Where do you work?; I work in a shop.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What does your mother do?  
> B: She is a teacher.  
> A: Where does she work?  
> B: She works at a school.

**Teaching notes:** Teach job nouns with workplace collocations. Avoid implying that any job is restricted to one gender.

**Communicative task:** Match job cards to workplace pictures, then describe two people.

## Lesson 2 — At School and in Class

**Can-do:** I can name familiar school subjects and understand basic classroom instructions.

**Core vocabulary:** English, maths, science, history, art, music, lesson, classroom, board, book, notebook, pen, pencil, teacher, student.

**Language focus:** My favourite subject is English; Open your book; Listen and repeat; I have maths on Monday.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What is your favourite subject?  
> B: I like science.  
> A: Do you have science today?  
> B: No, I don't.

**Teaching notes:** Subject names and school timetables vary by country. Use locally familiar examples and practise classroom imperatives with gestures.

**Communicative task:** Read a simple timetable and say which subjects are on two different days.

## Lesson 3 — Hobbies and Free-time Activities

**Can-do:** I can name common hobbies and say what I like doing.

**Core vocabulary:** read, draw, paint, cook, swim, dance, sing, cycle, play football, play tennis, listen to music, watch films, take photos.

**Language focus:** I like reading; He likes swimming; Do you like music?; Yes, I do / No, I don't.

**Model dialogue:**
> A: What do you do in your free time?  
> B: I listen to music and read books.  
> A: Do you like swimming?  
> B: Yes, I do.

**Teaching notes:** Introduce like + -ing as a useful chunk. Recycle familiar verbs and avoid overloading learners with spelling rules for gerunds.

**Communicative task:** Complete a simple hobby survey and report one classmate's preference.

## Lesson 4 — Days and Weekly Activities

**Can-do:** I can name the days and say when a weekly activity happens.

**Core vocabulary:** Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, weekday, weekend, today, tomorrow, every day.

**Language focus:** on Monday; at the weekend; I study English on Friday; What day is it today?

**Model dialogue:**
> A: When do you have English?  
> B: I have English on Tuesday and Thursday.  
> A: Do you study at the weekend?  
> B: Yes, I do.

**Teaching notes:** Week starts and weekend conventions differ across regions. Accept local calendars; the English names of the seven days remain the target vocabulary.

**Communicative task:** Ask a partner about two weekly activities and complete a simple schedule.

## Lesson 5 — Talking About Work and Study

**Can-do:** I can ask and answer simple questions about where people work or study.

**Core vocabulary:** work, study, office, school, shop, at home, every day, usually, sometimes, do, does.

**Language focus:** Where do you work?; Where does she study?; I don't work on Sunday; He doesn't study French; Do they work here?

**Model dialogue:**
> A: Do you work or study?  
> B: I study English.  
> A: Where do you study?  
> B: I study at a language school.

**Teaching notes:** Contrast do/does in questions with the base verb after does/doesn't. Keep adverbs of frequency to a small receptive set; teach their placement in short, clear examples.

**Communicative task:** Use a simple profile card to ask and answer three questions about work or study.
